Digital Citizenship

What is Digital Citizenship?

Digital citizenship refers to the ability to navigate digital environments in a safe and responsible way while actively and respectfully engaging in these spaces. It encompasses the knowledge, skills, and mindsets required to use technology ethically and effectively.

What is Digital Law?

Digital law is the legal framework governing electronic actions and the use of digital technology. It establishes the "electronic responsibility" for deeds performed online, distinguishing between ethical uses—such as properly citing sources—and unethical or illegal acts like hacking, pirating, and cyberbullying.

What is AUP?

In 2026, an Acceptable Use Policy (AUP) is a foundational document that outlines the rules and guidelines for how individuals—including employees, students, and contractors—can interact with an organization's digital resources

What is Plagiarism?

In 2026, plagiarism is defined as the act of using another person's work, ideas, or intellectual property without proper attribution and presenting them as one's own. It is considered a form of "literary theft" or intellectual fraud that undermines academic and professional integrity.

What is Academic Dishonesty?

In 2026, academic dishonesty (also known as academic misconduct) is an umbrella term for any fraudulent or deceptive act used to gain an unfair advantage in an educational setting. While often associated with students, it applies to anyone involved in research, teaching, or learning who violates the ethical standards of their academic community.

What are the rules for AI Generated Content?

In 2026, the rules for AI-generated content (AIGC) are governed by a combination of new laws (like the EU AI Act and California state laws), strict academic integrity policies, and evolving copyright standards.
